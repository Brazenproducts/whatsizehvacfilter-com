How to Read HVAC Filter Size Labels

The easiest filter decision is the one where you trust the label on the old filter. The trouble is that many people look at the wrong number, mix up actual and nominal size, or assume a faded stamp means they need to start guessing. You do not.

Most residential filters tell you everything you need to know right on the cardboard frame. You just need to know what you are looking at.

What the Three Numbers Mean

Typical residential HVAC filters are listed as three measurements, in inches:

  • Width — side to side across the face of the filter
  • Height — top to bottom across the face
  • Depth — thickness of the filter

A filter labeled 20x25x1 is the common example. That means the filter is sold as 20 inches by 25 inches by 1 inch thick. The first two numbers describe the face. The last number describes the thickness.

Orientation is not the hard part

If the first two numbers are flipped, that is usually not a problem. A 16x25x1 and a 25x16x1 are the same physical filter. The key is that the face dimensions and thickness match your system.

Nominal Size vs. Actual Size

This is where people get tripped up. HVAC filters are sold by nominal size, which is the rounded name of the filter. The actual physical size is slightly smaller so the filter can slide into the track without binding.

If your old filter shows both sizes, use the larger, cleaner whole-number size for ordering. If you see decimals, those are usually the actual measurements for reference only.

Where the Size Is Printed

On most filters, the size is printed directly on the outer frame. Check these spots:

  • The front cardboard edge you can see when pulling the filter out
  • The narrow side edge if the front print has faded
  • A product label or barcode sticker attached to the frame

If the filter has arrows, those arrows indicate airflow direction, not the size order. Do not confuse the arrow direction with width and height.

If the Print Is Worn Off

That does not mean you have to guess. Pull the filter out and measure the old filter itself with a tape measure.

  1. Measure across the face from side to side.
  2. Measure top to bottom on the face.
  3. Measure the thickness from front to back.
  4. Round those dimensions to the nominal size used in stores.

Example: if the filter measures roughly 19.5 x 24.5 x 0.75 inches, you buy a 20x25x1.

Do not buy by exact decimal measurement

Trying to shop for a 19.5 x 24.5 x 0.75 filter is the fastest way to confuse yourself. Retail and direct-to-consumer stores list filters by nominal size.

1-Inch and 4-Inch Filters Are Different Categories

Many homes use 1-inch filters in a return grille or a simple filter slot near the furnace. Other systems use deeper media cabinets that take 4-inch or 5-inch filters. Thickness is not optional.

If your current filter is a 20x25x4, a 20x25x1 is not a substitute. The face may match, but the housing is different and the filter will not seal the same way.

What to Buy Once You Know the Size

After size, the next useful choice is filtration level. For most homes, a pleated filter in MERV 8, 10, or 13 covers the practical range. That is why we like brands that make sizing simple and keep the rating choices straightforward.
